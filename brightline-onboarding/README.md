# Brightline Onboarding

Priya Raghavan's technical onboarding, guided by the assistant. A concept prototype on the Brightline story's data spine: the Developer Portal the week after Dana named her, with the assistant as the Getting started page. She describes what she is connecting; the assistant reads it against the deal, draws the integration, starts the things other people hold on day one, and prepares every step for her approval. Time passes on a clock the viewer moves. Nothing moves without her.

Built to [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) on November 22, 2026, on the Brightline Assist engine, then re-planned and rebuilt the same day to [PLAN-v2.md](PLAN-v2.md), which follows the v2 flow in `~/Desktop/Claude/Agent Assist Research/Engineer-Onboarding-Flow-Plan.md`: how an acquirer actually gets onto a network, with two tracks, waits, a build step, a back office, people, and a ramp instead of a switch.

## How it runs

- **Getting started** is the ask alone: a headline, the field, one chip that types Brightline's description, and one quiet line of what the deal already settled. Nothing is drawn until she writes. There is no sidebar: nothing behind it exists until she is through.
- **The route.** The answer to her description is the route: a lead that says how many steps on two tracks and what was assumed, and four figures: weeks to the first ramp step, steps, how many things are out with other people and which lands next, and May 14 with "on track" or the days at risk. The step rail on the left carries the route from then on. (The integration drawing, her systems paired with the network's, was retired Nov 23: its lines repeated their labels and its states repeated the rail. Choices change by saying so in the bar.)
- **Surfaces.** Only what waits on her or floats is a surface: the prepared action with the Ring, the bar, the rail, the pane, the sheet. The turn head and the evidence sit on the page; turns are divided by one rule.
- **Two tracks.** The build track is hers: the first call, her agents, mapping the authorization messages, events, the back office, configuration, certification, readiness, the ramp. The long-lead track is other people's moves, started on **day one**: the allowlist and the production credentials and a certification window hold from Marcus Bell at the network, the certificate chain from her CA through her security team, the firewall change from her own network team. Day one sends three requests, one at a time through the sheet, and the checklist re-draws with owner and due date after each.
- **The clock.** The date sits in the header, "Tuesday, Mar 10 · day 1". Time moves only when the viewer moves it: the date opens a menu with "Skip ahead to Mar 19 · the chain is issued", the next move among everything out with other people. Skipping re-draws the route and opens a return briefing, "Since Mar 10": what moved, as rows with who, and what is still to come, then the next step prepared. The margin shows the same drawing small, "Step 7 of 11 · about 4 weeks left · first ramp step May 14", and up to three of their moves with dates.
- **Waits are turns.** When a step cannot start because the next move is someone else's, the turn says whose and since when, lists the moves with their dues, offers what she can do meanwhile on the other track, and carries a Nudge button with a drafted note. Securing the connection waits on the allowlist, the chain and the firewall change; readiness waits on production credentials; certification waits on Ana Okafor's review; each ramp step waits on the next morning's settlement file.
- **Before you decide asks what informs the decision.** Under a prepared action the chips are questions whose answers bear on it: why the long-lead items take so long, what the key can never do, what missed in the mapping, how a settlement line maps to a ledger entry, what the handshake proves, what the cases are, what Ana said, what happens on the first morning, what the thresholds are. The next steps appear only after the receipt.
- **The hard moments.** Mapping: 41 scenarios in five groups run from her agent, 3 miss on one field, the rerun passes. Connectivity: the handshake fails on a chain one link short; the assistant shows the expected chain and prepares the corrected upload. Certification: three cases fail on a rounding rule, one value fixes them; round 1 goes to Ana, her two comments come back as rows, round 2 is clean, the window Apr 20 to 24 is confirmed.
- **Launch is a ramp.** The pilot cohort is one merchant, Marlow & Third, from 6:00 AM May 14; then 10%, 50% and 100%, each prepared only after the previous step's first settlement file reconciles. The measures sit against the gateway's baseline with the four thresholds; "Reroute to the gateway" is always on screen. Hypercare to Jun 9; the gateway retires Sep 23. The hand-off is the Developer Portal's first ask: "What failed overnight?"
- **Honesty.** MCP is for building and operating: her agents read the reference, run the scenarios, send test cards, read logs, run certification, and can watch the sandbox stream while she builds. It never carries the live authorization leg, and production events reach her by webhook or by polling.
- **She can type at any point.** The bar understands "skip ahead", "what am I waiting on", "nudge Marcus", "how long", "python", "we poll", the name of a step, and says what it did.

Controls follow the Brightline Assist rule: anything said to the assistant is a violet chip; acts are buttons, Heritage for the one commit on screen, tonal for the rest; world changes go through the platform's confirm sheet and return a receipt with an undo or recall window; her own settings change in place. Exit is the close button on the assist bar; the thread is kept and the page offers to continue it.

## Files

- `index.html`, `onboard.css`, `onboard-v5.css`, `onboard.js`, `data.js`, `data-v2.js`, `docs.js`. The look is `onboard-v5.css`, loaded after `onboard.css`: Design System Labs tokens (`tokens/colors.css`), Proxima Nova (`fonts/`), no gradients, white page, nothing under 14px. Three regions in a turn view: the step rail fixed left (`drawRail`), the column centred, and the reading pane fixed right when a reference is open (`openPane`, pages in `docs.js`, mapped to turns by `refs`). Each turn is a head card (`.thead`: summary, title, said, lead, figure, references), then the prepared action and the asks, then the evidence cards; CSS `order` keeps that sequence whatever order the engine appends in. Option charts are HTML bars (`barsHTML`).
- The engine data (route state, the clock, the long-lead items, the steps; `map()` still feeds the wait line for the connection) and the v1 content are in `data.js`; the v2 arc's turns and acts are in `data-v2.js`, assembled from seven drafted parts (day one, the first call, mapping, events and the back office, secure and configuration, certification and readiness, the ramp), each wrapped in `(function(D){ … })(window.OB)` and loaded after `data.js`. Text that depends on the route is a function of it.
- `dist/artifact.html`: the page without the document skeleton, for publishing as a claude.ai artifact.
- `design-system/icons.js`: the demo's icon set. `img/`: Priya's portrait.
- `PLAN.md`: the first plan and the record of versions 1 to 12. `PLAN-v2.md`: the re-plan against the v2 flow, Rich's four decisions, and what was built.

Serve the folder statically (launch config `brightline-onboarding`, port 8101). Add `?fast` to skip the motion. The test hook drives the demo to any state: `?auto=<turn>&today=2026-04-07&visited=2026-04-02&done=d0,s1&acts=reqnet,csr&long=all&rounds=1&ramp=10`. "Start the route over" is in the date menu; "Sign out" in the person menu.

Everything in it is invented: Brightline Bank, the people, every figure, the endpoints and keys.
